In the US, some people have very traditional wedding ceremonies, but others prefer 
nontraditional ceremonies. Many people get married in places like event centers or even at 
home. Some weddings are in the morning, but most weddings are in the afternoon or the 
evening. If you receive a wedding invitation, you should send an RSVP. You can do this online
if the wedding couple has a wedding website. There is usually an online gift registry so that you 
can send gifts directly to the bride and groom. Guests at traditional weddings usually wear suits
and dresses. People also dress up for nontraditional weddings, but the clothes are usually a little 
less formal.

On the day of the wedding, the bride and groom do not see each other before the ceremony.
In traditional weddings, the bride walks to the groom with her father, but now some brides walk 
with bothparents. After the ceremony, there is a wedding reception, where there’s a big meal, 
and friends and family members give short speeches. There is usually a band or a DJ, and 
people dance and celebrate through the night with the newly married couple.

Social Customs in the US

When you travel to another country, it is important to
understand the customs of the country. Here are some
useful tips for visitors to the US.

GREETINGS

•When Americans meet someone for the first time, 
especially in business situations, they usually shake 
hands. Young people do not usually shake hands in 
informal situations. Kissing on the cheek is nota 
common form of greeting.

•At small social events, Americans sometimes greet 
each person, but normally people say hello to the 
group in general.

•Use a person’s title (Mr., Ms., Dr.) and last name in
business situations or when the other person is much 
older than you. Never use a title with a first name.

SOCIAL EVENTS

•Do not accept an invitation to an event if you don’t plan
to go. Simply say something like, “I’m sorry, but I have 
plans that evening. Thanks for the invitation.”

In this lesson, students learn how to think critically  
about their own culture and give advice about social  
customs in their country. Students have to consider  
social customs they are familiar with in a new way. They 
look at themselves from the point of view of an outsider 
and practice empathy for someone visiting their country.

Students ﬁrst read an excerpt from a guidebook about 
social customs in the United States. Then, they are 
presented with a task. An American friend’s email outlines 
his upcoming visit and his questions about social customs 
in the students’ country. Students work in pairs to think 
of answers to his questions and then consider any other 
important customs in their country they think visitors 
should be aware of. Then, in groups, they share their 
information and add details to it. Finally, they discuss any 
points of disagreement.
