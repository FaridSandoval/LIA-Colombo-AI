up with creative ideas for improving public health. They 
are encouraged to think critically about the problem of 
obesity and poor food choices and suggest possible 
solutions. Students then choose the best ideas. They will 
discuss why some ideas will not work.

Students ﬁrst read an article about public health, obesity, 
and poor food choices. The article outlines some 
solutions being implemented, such as mandatory food 
labeling and government health education programs. 
Students will review ways to brainstorm. In groups, 
students brainstorm ways to improve the health of the 
population. The activity asks for solutions for three 
different target groups within the population. The 
groups then decide which ideas won’t work and why. 
They choose the best ideas and explain why they will 
work. Finally, they present their best ideas to the class 
and predict what would happen if the public health 
department used their ideas.
