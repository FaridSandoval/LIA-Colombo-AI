In this lesson, students learn how to evaluate personal 
qualities and achievements to decide who should receive 
an award for being a hero. They use what they have 
learned about role models to judge the merits of people 
who have done extraordinary things and propose other 
people who deserve awards.

Students ﬁrst read three short articles that discuss 
people who have done extraordinary things, overcome 
disabilities, raised money for others, saved others from 
dangerous situations, or helped the community. Then, 
they analyze why the featured people are heroes. Based 
on the examples in the articles, students research other 
people who have done extraordinary things and make a 
list. From the list, they make proposals for citizens’ awards. 
Using the criteria they have been considering for what 
makes a hero, students choose people from their list to 
recognize in an awards ceremony. Finally, students  
role-play an awards ceremony and discuss questions.

or seen. As they are talking about 
someone else, make sure they 
use the third person. Give a quick 
overview of some of the grammar 
they will use in the past tense. For 
example, He/She was [X]ing when  
he/she won the award.

Meet 20-year-old Victor Karlsson from Malmo, Sweden. 
When Victor was eight years old, he became blind in an
accident. With help from his family and friends, Victor is 
now a local hero in Malmo. Victor and his guide dog Charlie 
are helping improve the lives of blind children and adults by 
raising money. In 2018, Victor raised 1,500 euros walking 
111 km between Malmo and Copenhagen. In 2020, he is 
planning to run a half-marathon. “Everyone looks up to 
Victor,” says his friend Katya. “He’s a true role model.”

Nineteen-year-old Petra Gonzalez saved her sister from a fire 
in their home in Mexico. Petra, a university student, woke up 
one night to the smell of smoke. She woke up her sister. She 
was carrying her sister out of their apartment when she saw 
the fire on the other side of the house. Amazingly, firefighters
arrived in time to save both girls. Petra got an award from the 
local mayor. “I was really scared,” said Petra, “but I was just 
doing what any sister would do.”

While Mimi Kallas was working in a tourist office in her 
home town in Greece, she noticed men and women on the 
streets asking for food. Mimi stopped working at her job
and started a food service for homeless people. She collects 
extra food from stores and cooks up to 100 meals a day in the
kitchen of a school. She gives out the food for free to anyone 
who needs it. “Without Mimi, we couldn’t survive,” said one
man. “She is an amazing woman. I’m so glad she is here to 
help us.”
