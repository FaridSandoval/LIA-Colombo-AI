eighborhoods are changing all the time. That means

It might be urban planners or members of local 
government. They often have to decide between different 
plans for a neighborhood. They may have to think about 
costs, housing, and the needs of local people.  
How do they make their decisions?
One tool to help planners make a decision is a
decision matrix. In a decision matrix, they list the 
important results they want for the neighborhood. For 
example, local people may want more houses, less traffic, 
or more parks. Planners develop severalplans toget 
these results. They then score each plan against each 
result: –2 for a very negative effect, –1 for a negative 
effect, 0 for no effect, +1 for a positive effect, and +2 for 
a very positive effect. The plan with the highest total 
score is the one to choose.

In this lesson, students learn how to use a decision matrix, 
which is a table that helps in identifying, evaluating, 
and rating facts related to a problem. It allows the user 
to prioritize options based on criteria in a methodical, 
mathematical way. This promotes objectivity when 
confronted with a problem to solve.

Students ﬁrst read an article about who makes the 
decisions for urban development, some of the decisions 
that have to be made, and how they do it. Then, groups 
choose criteria they think are most important to people 
living in a community. Their choices go in the decision 
matrix. In pairs, students read three proposals for a 
community. Next, they score each line of the matrix, 
comparing the criteria to the plans. The total score says 
which plan best matches with what people want. Finally, 
they compare their results on the decision matrix in  with 
another pair’s, and discuss their results.

One example is half-houses in Chile. An earthquake hit the
town of Constitución in 2010 and destroyed about 80% of the 
buildings. The local government hired an architecture firm to 
provide low-costthouses. They built houses that are only half-
finished. The owners finish the house when they have the money. 
It means that each family can decide what they need: a garage, 
another bedroom, a hall, a balcony. One resident says: “We’re
going to build another bedroom and bathroom in two years. We 
can add our personal style.”
Another solution is micro-units, like those at Carmel Place in 
New York City. These are very small apartments, around 28
square meters. They are too small for a family, but perfect for 
single young professionals. Each one-room apartment has the 
usual appliances—a stove, a refrigerator, and a microwave—and 
there’s a room in another part of the building for washing clothes. 
The smart use of special furniture means the room can become 
a bedroom, kitchen, office, or living room. The desk becomes a
dining table and the couch becomes a bed. The coffee table folds
down and goes under the bed at night. A young resident says: 
“I’m having a friend over for dinner tonight. I just turn the bed
into a couch, the desk into a table, and we’re good to go!”
