# Unit 10 — Wellbeing

## Lesson 1 — Power Up Your Brain!

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Brain Power**
Read the definitions to learn words related to how your mind works and how to keep it healthy:
- **diet:** all the things you usually eat (noun).
- **daily routine:** the things that happen to you each day (noun).
- **puzzles:** games or tasks where you have to think carefully to find the answer (noun).
- **cells:** the smallest basic units of your brain and body (noun).
- **intelligent:** smart; able to think very clearly about things (adjective).
- **brain:** the organ inside your head that controls your body (noun).
- **memory:** the part of you that remembers things (noun).
- **nuts:** the dry fruit of some trees that have a hard shell, which are good for brain health (noun).

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: zero_and_first_conditional -->

**Zero and First Conditional**
We use conditional sentences to talk about causes and results.
- **Zero Conditional:** Used for facts and things that are generally true. 
  *Form:* If + simple present, simple present.
  *Example:* **If** you **challenge** your brain, it **builds** new connections.
- **First Conditional:** Used for possible future situations and their probable results.
  *Form:* If + simple present, *will* + base verb.
  *Example:* **If** you **eat** healthy food, you **will improve** your memory.

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Choose the correct option to complete the conditional sentences:
   a. If people (don't sleep / won't sleep) enough, they can't think clearly the next day.
   b. If you play puzzles and learn new skills, you (become / will become) more intelligent.
   c. If you (eat / will eat) a lot of sugar, it is bad for your brain cells.
   d. She (will do / does) better on her exam tomorrow if she goes to bed early tonight.

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Reading Skill - Scan:** Have students scan the article "3 Ways to Power Up Your Brain" to find the main tips (Get healthy, Sleep better, Keep learning). Ask them to quickly check ideas mentioned in the text without reading every word.
**Thinking Skill - Brainstorm:** Have students brainstorm other ways they could develop their brain power (e.g., meditating, exercising, reading).
**Pronunciation:** Focus on the intonation in conditional sentences. Explain that the voice usually goes up at the end of the *if*-clause, and down at the end of the main clause.

### Answer Key
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: answer_key -->

**Practice (Zero and First Conditional):**
a. don't sleep (Zero Conditional - general fact)
b. will become (First Conditional - future result)
c. eat (Zero Conditional - general fact)
d. will do (First Conditional - future result)

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

In many modern cultures, there is a heavy emphasis on "brain training" apps and continuous learning to prevent cognitive decline. However, recent science often points back to basics: good sleep, regular exercise, and a balanced diet (like the Mediterranean diet) are the most effective ways to maintain brain health.

## Lesson 2 — Accidents and Illnesses

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Accidents and Illnesses**
Words and phrases to describe health problems:
- **break a bone:** to snap or fracture a hard part of the body (like an arm or leg).
- **burn your hand:** to injure your hand with fire or heat.
- **sprain your ankle:** to twist and injure the joint connecting the foot to the leg.
- **have a cold:** a common illness that makes you cough, sneeze, and have a runny nose.
- **get a virus:** to become infected with an illness caused by a virus.
- **have a temperature / fever:** to have a higher body temperature than normal because you are sick.

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: second_conditional -->

**Second Conditional**
We use the second conditional to talk about imaginary, hypothetical, or unlikely situations in the present or future, and their results.
- *Form:* If + simple past, *would* (or *could/might*) + base verb.
- *Example:* **If** I **had** a broken leg, I **would go** to the hospital. (I don't have a broken leg right now, so I am imagining it).
- *Note:* In the *if*-clause, it is common to use **were** for all subjects (e.g., *If I were you...*).

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Complete the second conditional sentences with the correct form of the verbs in parentheses:
   a. If he __________ (burn) his hand, he __________ (put) it under cold water.
   b. If I __________ (be) you, I __________ (not / go) to work with a fever.
   c. What __________ (you / do) if you __________ (sprain) your ankle on a hike?
   d. She __________ (feel) better if she __________ (take) some medicine.

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Listening Skill - Listen for cause and effect:** Have students listen to an interview with a health expert. Ask them to identify the causes of certain injuries and the recommended treatments.
**Speaking:** Have students role-play a conversation about illnesses. One student is sick or injured, and the other asks about the illness and gives advice using the second conditional (*"If I were you, I would..."*).
**Confident Communicator:** Teach phrases to state agreement or disagreement politely when discussing health remedies (e.g., *I completely agree. / I'm not sure that works.*).

### Answer Key
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: answer_key -->

**Practice (Second Conditional):**
a. burned (or burnt) / would put
b. were / wouldn't go (would not go)
c. would you do / sprained
d. would feel / took

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

Home remedies for common illnesses vary wildly around the world. While someone in the US might drink chicken noodle soup for a cold, someone in Latin America might drink an herbal tea with honey and lemon, or ginger. Discussing these remedies is a great way to practice the second conditional (*If I had a cold, my grandmother would make...*).

## Lesson 3 — Getting in Shape

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Collocations with *Get***
The verb *get* is very common in English and can mean become, receive, or arrive. Here are health-related collocations:
- **get in shape:** to become physically fit by exercising.
- **get sick:** to become ill.
- **get better:** to recover from an illness or injury.
- **get tired:** to start feeling that you need to rest or sleep.
- **get injured:** to suffer physical harm or damage to your body.
- **get enough sleep:** to sleep the appropriate amount of hours for good health.

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: writing_website_comment -->

*(Note: In this lesson, the focus shifts towards Writing and Speaking, using conditionals and collocations to express opinions).*

**Making Statements Stronger**
When writing a website comment or expressing an opinion about health, you can make your statements stronger by using adverbs of degree.
- *I **strongly** believe that nutrition is more important than exercise.*
- *It is **extremely** difficult to get in shape without a good diet.*
- *I am **absolutely** sure that getting enough sleep improves your memory.*

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Complete the sentences with the correct *get* collocation:
   a. If you exercise three times a week, you will __________.
   b. Wash your hands often during winter so you don't __________.
   c. He had a bad cold last week, but he is starting to __________.
   d. If you don't warm up before playing sports, you might __________.

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Writing:** Have students read an online article about small life changes (like drinking more water or taking the stairs). Then, ask them to write a website comment responding to the article. They should state their opinion, make their statements stronger using adverbs, and use conditionals.
**Language and Life - Critical Thinking:** Set up a collaborative task where students create campaigns to improve public health in their school or community. They must choose whether to focus on mental health, nutrition, or physical fitness.
**Follow A Pro:** If using the video, have students watch a family physician or physical therapist to learn about careers in the healthcare industry.

### Answer Key
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: answer_key -->

**Practice (Collocations with get):**
a. get in shape
b. get sick
c. get better
d. get injured

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

The concept of "getting in shape" is heavily promoted in media globally, but what is considered "in shape" or "healthy" can be culturally subjective. Some cultures emphasize being thin, while others emphasize strength or overall physical capability.
