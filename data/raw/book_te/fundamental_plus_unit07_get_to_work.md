# Unit 7 — Get to Work

## Lesson 1 — Career Readiness

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Career Readiness**
Read the definitions to learn vocabulary related to starting your professional life:
- **degree:** a qualification given for completing a university course.
- **career:** a job or profession that someone does for a long time.
- **skills:** the abilities to do an activity or job well.
- **organized:** able to plan things carefully and keep things in order.
- **apply:** to make a formal request, usually in writing, for a job.
- **interview:** a formal meeting where someone is asked questions to see if they are suitable for a job.
- **bonus:** extra money added to a person's pay as a reward for good performance.

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: present_perfect_already_yet_just -->

**Present Perfect with *already*, *yet*, and *just***
We use the present perfect to talk about recent actions or life experiences. We often use specific adverbs with it:
- **just:** used for very recent events. It goes between *have/has* and the past participle.
- **already:** used to say something happened sooner than expected (affirmative sentences). It goes between *have/has* and the past participle.
- **yet:** used to ask if something has happened, or to say it hasn't happened (questions and negative sentences). It goes at the end of the sentence.

*Examples:*
- I have **just** graduated from a good university.
- I have **already** made a new website.
- I haven't started my new job **yet**.

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Complete the sentences with the present perfect form of the verbs and adverbs in parentheses:
   a. I'm so excited because I 1 __________ (just / finish) my degree!
   b. I 2 __________ (not / start) my new job yet.
   c. I 3 __________ (already / put) my new website online, but I haven't had many orders yet.
   d. Have you 4 __________ (write / yet) your business plan?

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Vocabulary:** Discuss career readiness. Ask students what skills they think are necessary for their dream jobs. Use the vocabulary words to discuss the process of getting a job (e.g., *first you get a degree, then you apply, then you have an interview*).
**Grammar Activity:** Have students write three things they have *already* done today, three things they haven't done *yet*, and one thing they have *just* done. Emphasize the position of the adverbs in the sentence structure.
**Thinking Skill - Summarize:** Have students read a short informative article about career readiness and summarize the key employability skills mentioned.

### Answer Key
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: answer_key -->

**Practice (Present Perfect with adverbs):**
a. 1 have just finished
b. 2 haven't started
c. 3 have already put
d. 4 written your business plan yet

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

The concept of a "bonus" and how performance is rewarded can vary by country. In many English-speaking corporate cultures, performance-based bonuses are a standard part of career progression, emphasizing personal achievement and hitting specific targets.

## Lesson 2 — Employability Skills & Success

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Employability Skills**
Words to describe skills and characteristics that make you a good worker:
- **hard-working:** putting a lot of effort into a job.
- **communication:** the ability to share ideas and information clearly.
- **problem-solving:** the process of finding solutions to difficult or complex issues.
- **creative:** producing or using original and unusual ideas.
- **team player:** someone who works well with other people.

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: present_perfect_vs_simple_past -->

**Present Perfect vs. Simple Past**
- Use the **Simple Past** when you mention a specific, finished time in the past (e.g., *yesterday, last week, in 2020*).
- Use the **Present Perfect** when the exact time is not important or not mentioned, or when talking about an action that has a result in the present.

*Examples:*
- I **wrote** my business plan *yesterday*. (Simple Past - specific time)
- I **have written** my business plan. (Present Perfect - time not specified, the plan is ready now)

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Choose the correct tense (Present Perfect or Simple Past) to complete the sentences:
   a. I (was / have been) very busy my first week of work last month.
   b. She (didn't check / hasn't checked) the job listings yesterday.
   c. They (already finished / have already finished) their training program.
   d. He (started / has started) his own business in 2023.

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Vocabulary:** Ask students to evaluate their own employability skills. Which ones are their strongest? Which ones do they need to improve? 
**Grammar:** To help students distinguish between the two tenses, write time markers on the board (e.g., *last year, already, just, two days ago, yet*) and have students categorize them under "Simple Past" or "Present Perfect".
**Speaking - Confident Communicator:** Teach students how to give negative answers politely during an interview context (e.g., instead of just saying "No," saying "I haven't had experience with that yet, but I am a fast learner").

### Answer Key
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: answer_key -->

**Practice (Present Perfect vs. Simple Past):**
a. was
b. didn't check
c. have already finished
d. started

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

In job interviews in places like the US or the UK, candidates are often expected to explicitly state their achievements and be confident about their "employability skills." In some other cultures, this level of self-promotion might be seen as arrogant. Discussing this difference helps prepare students for international careers.

## Lesson 3 — Writing a Personal Statement

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Success and Qualifications**
Terms used when applying for jobs and programs:
- **personal statement:** a written description of your achievements, interests, and goals, usually included in an application.
- **qualifications:** exams you have passed or courses you have completed.
- **achievements:** things you have done successfully, especially through hard work.
- **entrepreneur:** someone who starts their own business.

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: narrative_review -->

*(Note: In this lesson, the focus shifts towards Writing and Speaking, using the grammar from the previous lessons to present oneself professionally).*

**Describing Your Experience**
When writing a personal statement or speaking in an interview, combine the Simple Past and Present Perfect:
- Use the *Simple Past* to talk about your completed education or specific past roles: *I graduated from a good university last year.*
- Use the *Present Perfect* to talk about your accumulated experience and current status: *I have just finished my degree, and I am ready to start my career.*

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Organize the following notes into a logical order for a personal statement (1 to 4):
   a. My future goals and why I want this job. _____
   b. A summary of my current status (e.g., I have just graduated). _____
   c. My contact information. _____
   d. Details about my specific qualifications and past achievements. _____

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Writing:** Have students write a short personal statement (about 100 words). They should include their education, their skills, and what kind of career they are looking for. Tell them to use the present perfect to highlight recent accomplishments.
**Language and Life - Problem Solving:** Set up a collaborative task where students act as consultants to create a basic business plan for a young entrepreneur. They must identify potential problems and suggest solutions.
**Pronunciation:** Focus on the /j/ and /dʒ/ sounds (e.g., *yet, just, job, year*). Have students practice these sounds in pairs to ensure they don't confuse words like "yellow" and "Jell-O".

### Answer Key
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: answer_key -->

**Practice (Personal Statement):**
1. c (Contact info)
2. b (Current status summary)
3. d (Qualifications/achievements)
4. a (Future goals)

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

A "personal statement" or "cover letter" is a critical part of the job application process in many English-speaking countries. It is just as important as the CV/Resume because it shows the employer the candidate's personality, communication skills, and motivation.
