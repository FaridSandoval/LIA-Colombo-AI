# Unit 11 — Use and Reuse

## Lesson 1 — The Lifecycle of a T-Shirt

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Product Lifecycles & Clothing Materials**
Read the definitions to learn words related to how clothes are made and processed:
- **cotton:** a plant used to make soft fabric for clothes (noun).
- **manufacture:** to make something in large quantities using machines (verb).
- **transport:** to move goods from one place to another (verb).
- **consume:** to buy and use things (verb).
- **dispose of:** to throw something away or get rid of it (verb).
- **recycle:** to process used objects so that they can be used again (verb).
- **spin:** to twist fibers like cotton together to make thread (verb).
- **weave:** to make cloth by crossing threads over and under each other (verb).

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: simple_present_passive -->

**Simple Present Passive**
We use the passive voice when the action is more important than who does the action, or when we don't know who does it.
- **Form:** subject + *is/are* + past participle.
- **Active:** Farmers **grow** cotton in many countries.
- **Passive:** Cotton **is grown** in many countries.

*Examples:*
- The T-shirts **are manufactured** in Asia.
- The fabric **is woven** using large machines.
- **Are** these clothes **recycled**?

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Complete the sentences with the simple present passive form of the verbs in parentheses:
   a. Millions of T-shirts __________ (buy) every day around the world.
   b. The cotton __________ (pick) by hand in some places.
   c. The clothes __________ (transport) to stores by ship and truck.
   d. Water __________ (use) in large amounts to make denim.
   e. How many plastic bottles __________ (recycle) each year?

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Reading Skill - Understand words from context:** Have students read an article about the lifecycle of a T-shirt. Before checking dictionaries, ask them to guess the meaning of new words (like *spin* or *weave*) based on the surrounding sentences.
**Speaking - Confident Communicator:** Teach students how to order steps in a process when speaking (e.g., *First, the cotton is grown. Next, it is transported...*).
**Grammar Note:** Emphasize that the object of the active sentence becomes the subject of the passive sentence. Show them visually on the board how the sentence structure flips.

### Answer Key
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: answer_key -->

**Practice (Simple Present Passive):**
a. are bought
b. is picked
c. are transported
d. is used
e. are recycled

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

The global supply chain of "fast fashion" is a highly debated topic. A single T-shirt might involve cotton grown in the US or India, manufacturing in Bangladesh or Vietnam, and consumption in Europe or Latin America. Discussing this lifecycle raises awareness about globalization and sustainability.

## Lesson 2 — Accidental Discoveries

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Inventions and Materials**
Words to talk about discoveries and things we use:
- **invent:** to design or create something that has never existed before (verb).
- **discover:** to find information, a place, or an object, especially for the first time (verb).
- **plastic:** an artificial substance that can be shaped when soft into many different forms (noun).
- **sticky:** made of a substance that attaches to surfaces (adjective).
- **create:** to make something happen or exist (verb).
- **accidental:** happening by chance, without being planned (adjective).

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: simple_past_passive -->

**Simple Past Passive**
We use the simple past passive to talk about actions completed in the past when the focus is on the object or the result of the action.
- **Form:** subject + *was/were* + past participle.
- If we want to say who did the action, we use the word **by**.

*Examples:*
- Plastic **was invented** in 1907.
- Penicillin **was discovered by** Alexander Fleming.
- The first sticky notes **were created** accidentally.

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Rewrite the active sentences in the simple past passive voice. Include the *by* phrase if it is important:
   a. Alexander Fleming discovered penicillin in 1928.
      Penicillin _________________________________________.
   b. Someone invented the first plastic in the 19th century.
      The first plastic ____________________________________.
   c. Marie Curie discovered radium.
      Radium ___________________________________________.
   d. Workers built the bridge in 2010.
      The bridge ________________________________________.

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Listening Skill - Listen for key words:** Have students listen to an interview about accidental discoveries. Ask them to listen specifically for dates and names associated with the inventions.
**Speaking:** Have students discuss famous inventions and state who made them using the passive voice (e.g., *The telephone was invented by Alexander Graham Bell*). 
**Thinking Skill - Analyze:** Have students evaluate whether accidental discoveries are more important than planned inventions.

### Answer Key
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: answer_key -->

**Practice (Simple Past Passive):**
a. Penicillin was discovered by Alexander Fleming in 1928.
b. The first plastic was invented in the 19th century.
c. Radium was discovered by Marie Curie.
d. The bridge was built in 2010.

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

Many famous everyday items were accidental discoveries, including Post-it notes, the microwave oven, and potato chips. Discussing these accidents helps students realize that mistakes in science and innovation can sometimes lead to incredible success.

## Lesson 3 — Upcycling and Innovation

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Collocations Connected to Materials**
Learn these common phrases used when discussing the environment and recycling:
- **throw away:** to put something in the garbage.
- **single-use:** made to be used once and then thrown away (e.g., single-use plastic).
- **environmentally friendly:** not harmful to the environment.
- **upcycle:** to make new furniture, objects, etc., out of old or used things or waste material.
- **reuse:** to use something again, sometimes for a different purpose.
- **reduce waste:** to create less garbage.

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: precise_academic_language -->

*(Note: In this lesson, the focus shifts towards Listening, Speaking, and Study Skills).*

**Using Precise Language**
When speaking or writing about academic topics like innovation and the environment, try to use precise collocations instead of general words.
- *Instead of:* Don't put that in the trash.
- *Use:* Please don't **throw** that **away**.
- *Instead of:* Plastic that you use one time is bad.
- *Use:* **Single-use** plastic is not **environmentally friendly**.

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Match the halves to make precise collocations and phrases:
   a. throw
   b. single-use
   c. environmentally
   d. reduce
   e. upcycle
   
   1. friendly _____
   2. waste _____
   3. old materials _____
   4. plastic _____
   5. away _____

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Listening:** Listen to a lecture on upcycling items. Have students listen for the gist of the lecture: What is the main difference between recycling and upcycling? (Upcycling adds value to the old item).
**Language and Life - Innovation:** Set up a collaborative task where students suggest upcycling projects. Give them an object (e.g., an old tire, a broken skateboard, plastic bottles) and have them design an innovative way to reuse it.
**Study Skills:** Direct students to the study skills section on "Using Precise Language in Academic Writing". Emphasize that replacing basic words with academic collocations improves their writing score significantly on tests.

### Answer Key
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: answer_key -->

**Practice (Collocations):**
a. 5 (throw away)
b. 4 (single-use plastic)
c. 1 (environmentally friendly)
d. 2 (reduce waste)
e. 3 (upcycle old materials)

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

"Upcycling" has become a massive trend on social media platforms globally. Unlike recycling, which breaks materials down to their base form, upcycling relies on human creativity to turn "trash into treasure" (like turning glass bottles into lamps or old pallets into furniture).
