# Unit 1 — Extraordinary

## Lesson 1 — Role Models

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Role Models**
Read the definitions to learn vocabulary related to role models:
- **emigrated:** left your country to move to a different country (verb)
- **look up to:** admire; think someone is a good role model (phrasal verb)
- **improve:** make something better (verb)
- **revealed:** told a secret (verb)
- **insect:** a small animal with six legs and wings (noun)
- **successful:** achieving wealth, admiration, or popularity (adjective)
- **focus on:** pay attention to; concentrate on (phrasal verb)
- **role:** a part in a movie or a play (noun)

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: past_progressive -->

**Past Progressive**
The past progressive describes actions that began in the past and continued for some time. 
- It always includes the form **was/were**.
- In the past progressive, add **-ing** to the base form of the verb.

*Example:* While they **were living** there, her father died.

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Complete the conversation with the past progressive form of the verb in parentheses:
   Ben: I called you last night, but you didn't answer. What 1 _____ (you do)?
   Ashley: We 2 _____ (celebrate) my great-grandfather's 100th birthday.
   Ben: Really? One hundred! That's amazing. Was he your role model while you 3 _____ (grow) up?
   Ashley: Yes, definitely. He was my hero! He 4 _____ (open) presents when you called. Everyone 5 _____ (clap).
   Ben: I'm sure he had a great time yesterday.
   Ashley: I think so. We 6 _____ (not plan) for the party to end at midnight!

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Reading (Optional):** Explain that a role model is a person who is considered a good example for other people, and should be a person whose values are to be admired. He or she should be a positive influence.
**Thinking Skill - Evaluate:** Have students discuss the qualities and positive values of famous people they consider to be good role models. Have them consider that a poor role model is someone with negative qualities (greedy, self-interested, dishonest).
**Vocabulary Extra Practice:** Extend practice by asking students to write the words in sentences of their own. Then have them share their sentences in pairs.
**Grammar Additional Activity:** Play *What Were You Doing At...?* Students work in groups of four. Take turns rolling a die and asking "What were you doing at [X] o'clock yesterday?". Add follow-up questions to keep the conversation going.

### Answer Key
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: answer_key -->

**Practice (Past Progressive):**
1. were you doing
2. were celebrating
3. were growing
4. was opening
5. was clapping
6. weren't planning

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

The reading text features Lila Downs, a Mexican-American singer-songwriter from Oaxaca, Mexico, who sings in Spanish, English, and Mixtec. Highlighting figures like her can be a great way to discuss how role models come from diverse cultural backgrounds and make an impact through art and activism.

## Lesson 2 — Memorable Events

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Reactions**
Words to express reactions and feelings to different events:
- **disappointed:** sad because something was not as good as expected, or because something did not happen.
- **worried:** unhappy because you are thinking about problems or unpleasant things that might happen.
- **scared:** frightened or afraid.
- **thrilled:** very happy and excited.
- **glad:** pleased and happy.
- **confused:** unable to understand or think clearly.

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: past_progressive_vs_simple_past -->

**Past Progressive vs. Simple Past**
- The **past progressive** describes an action in progress.
- The **simple past** describes an interrupting action.
- We use **while** to introduce the action in progress.
- We use **when** to introduce the interrupting action.
- We use a comma after *when* and *while* clauses at the beginning of a sentence.

*Example:* I **was watching** the game at my friend's house *when* the phone **rang**.

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Complete the text with the correct form of the verb in parentheses. Use the past progressive or the simple past:
   Kiera: Did you hear that news story about the animal rescue?
   Asha: No. What 1 _____ (happen)?
   Kiera: I 2 _____ (read) about it on the way to work yesterday. 
   Asha: Yes, I remember it.
   Kiera: Well, after the hurricane, when people 3 _____ (leave) their homes, there were lots of pets that 4 _____ (live) on the streets. An airline organized a flight to take 60 of the animals to San Diego. I 5 _____ (cry) when I read the story. There was a photo of the pilot and he 6 _____ (hold) some of the puppies.

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Vocabulary Activity:** Have students practice the vocabulary by suggesting other scenarios in which they feel these emotions. Tell students that body language is important. Have them represent the emotions with gestures and research how gestures might differ across countries.
**Listening Additional Activity:** Investigate Vanessa Van Edwards's universal micro-expressions. Change emotions to the "I am / I feel" form (e.g., *I am happy, I feel disgusted*).
**Grammar Activity:** Ask students to talk about the last time they got injured using the target grammar. Example: "I was peeling a potato when I stabbed myself with a knife."
**Confident Communicator:** If students don't know a word, teach them to use a word that means something similar (e.g., *very worried* for *scared*) or give a short description (*a lot of rain* for *storm*).

### Answer Key
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: answer_key -->

**Practice (Past Progressive vs. Simple Past):**
1. happened
2. was reading
3. left
4. were living
5. was crying
6. was holding

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

When teaching reactions and body language, remind students that gestures can vary significantly from one culture to another. Encourage them to compare how people express being "thrilled" or "confused" in Latin America versus English-speaking countries.

## Lesson 3 — Coincidences

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Family Situations**
Words and phrases to describe family situations:
- **adopted:** to legally take another person's child into your own family and take care of him or her as your own child.
- **divorced:** no longer married.
- **engaged:** having formally agreed to marry.
- **large family:** a family with many members (e.g., many siblings).
- **only child:** a child who has no brothers or sisters.
- **single:** not married or not having a romantic relationship with someone.
- **single parent:** a person who takes care of their children on their own, without a husband, wife, or partner.
- **twins:** two children born at the same time to the same mother.

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: narrative_sequencing -->

**Sequencing Phrases for Anecdotes**
Use sequencing words and phrases to connect the events in an anecdote:
- *To start/set the time:* About three months ago... / Two weeks ago... / Last week...
- *To connect events:* After that... / Then... / Later that day... / A few days later...
- *To conclude:* In the end... / Finally...

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Complete the sentences with the vocabulary words:
   a. My sister and I are _____. She was born 10 minutes before I was.
   b. Jack comes from a _____. He has eight brothers and sisters.
   c. I am _____, but I want to get married in the future.
   d. I am an _____. When I was growing up, I wanted a brother or sister.
   e. My parents are _____. My mom got married again last year.
   f. Dan is a _____. His wife died five years ago, so he takes care of his kids on his own.
   g. We got _____ last year. We're getting married this summer.
   h. Lois's parents _____ her when she was six weeks old.

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Vocabulary:** Have students identify the stressed syllables in the vocabulary words and practice saying them with the correct stress. To keep conversation going, provide prompts like: *Where did you go on family vacations? What makes your family special?*
**Listening Skill - Predict the story:** When predicting what will happen in the second half of the anecdote, tell students to use clues from what they have heard in the first half. Accept all answers at this stage.
**Make it Real:** To show surprise in English, teach phrases like: *You're kidding! That's unbelievable! No way!* Have students practice saying these phrases until they are natural and spontaneous.
**Writing / Speaking:** Have students use time phrases to structure their writing and use transitional words to help the story flow. When speaking, encourage the use of facial expressions and gestures.

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

Family structures can vary widely. In Latin American cultures, "large families" might often include extended family members (grandparents, aunts, cousins) living close by or deeply involved in daily life, whereas in many English-speaking contexts, "family" immediately defaults to the nuclear family. Exploring these differences makes for a great conversation starter.
