# Unit 5 — Prepping

## Lesson 1 — Thanks for Your Help

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Checking In**
Words and phrases used for booking a hotel room and talking about hotel amenities:
- **People:** guests, bell boy, concierge, hotel receptionist.
- **Amenities & Services:** room service, wake-up call, hot water, shower, air conditioning, Wi-Fi, cable TV.
- **Room Items:** beds, pillows, ironing board, hair dryer, hotel key, hotel key card, room number.
- **Actions:** stay, book a room, check in.

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: modals_intention_offer_prediction -->

**Modals of intention, offer, and prediction**
- An **intention** is an aim or a desire for something to happen.
- To **offer** is to let someone know you will give them something if they want it.
- A **prediction** is a forecast or projection about what will happen in the future.
- For predictions, intentions, and offers, we can use the modal verb **will** (or **'ll**).

*Examples:*
- I **will** help you with your bags. (Offer)
- I think the food **will** be excellent. (Prediction)
- I **'ll** ask for an English menu. (Intention)

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Decide if the following sentences using *will* are an Intention (I), Offer (O), or Prediction (P):
   a. I'll carry that heavy suitcase for you. _____
   b. We'll probably arrive at the hotel around 8:00 p.m. _____
   c. I will call the concierge to ask for a wake-up call. _____
   d. The weather will be very hot during our stay. _____

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Reading / Thinking Skill - Predict:** Elicit what people are doing in the photos before reading. Students may not be able to tell immediately, but the first line of the conversation will give it away. Be open to all predictions at this point, as it is a good chance to activate schema.
**Speaking:** Have students review the conversation and role-play checking into a hotel. They may want to energize their conversations by adopting an attitude: they could be helpful or unhelpful, friendly or unfriendly, talkative or reserved.
**Career Note:** Hotel receptionists in different countries may behave in different and unexpected ways. As a guest, you should expect good service, but levels of friendliness may vary. Be ready to welcome people from many different countries and cultures, but be aware that physical contact and hand gestures may vary greatly.

### Answer Key
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: answer_key -->

**Practice (Modals of intention, offer, and prediction):**
a. Offer (O)
b. Prediction (P)
c. Intention (I)
d. Prediction (P)

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

Customer service expectations vary widely around the globe. In some countries, a concierge or receptionist is expected to be extremely formal and distant, while in others, a warm, conversational, and highly personalized approach is the standard.

## Lesson 2 — Planning a Party

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Making Suggestions**
Phrases to propose ideas for an event or plan:
- **Let's...** (e.g., Let's organize a class party.)
- **Why don't we...** (e.g., Why don't we order pizzas?)
- **How about...** (e.g., How about going to the park?)
- **What if we...** (e.g., What if we bring our own music?)

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: modals_request_permission -->

**Modals of Request and Permission**
We use modals to politely ask for things or ask if we can do something.
- **Requests (asking someone to do something for you):** Can you...?, Could you...?, Would you mind...-ing?
  *Example:* **Could** you help me decorate the room?
- **Permission (asking if you are allowed to do something):** Can I...?, Could I...?, May I...?
  *Example:* **May** I leave the party early?

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Complete the dialogue with a suitable modal or suggestion phrase:
   A: 1 __________ organizing a party for the end of the semester?
   B: That's a great idea. 2 __________ we use the school cafeteria?
   A: I'm not sure. 3 __________ you ask the principal for permission?
   B: Sure. 4 __________ order some food from the local restaurant, too.

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Vocabulary Activity:** Have students brainstorm different types of parties (birthday, graduation, class party) and list 5 suggestions for each using the target vocabulary.
**Grammar Activity:** Discuss the difference in formality between *Can*, *Could*, and *May*. Emphasize that *May* is typically used for more formal situations, while *Can* is informal. 
**Speaking:** Have students work in groups to plan a class party. They must use modals of request to ask group members to take on tasks (e.g., *Could you bring the drinks?*) and modals of permission.

### Answer Key
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: answer_key -->

**Practice (Suggestions & Modals):**
1. How about / What about
2. Can / Could / May
3. Can / Could
4. Let's / Why don't we

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

When making requests in English, being polite is very important. Using direct language like "Give me the food" is often considered rude. Using modals like "Could you please give me the food?" softens the request and is culturally expected in most English-speaking environments.

## Lesson 3 — Social Customs

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Wedding Customs**
Words and phrases related to weddings and formal social events:
- **bride:** the woman getting married.
- **groom:** the man getting married.
- **invitation:** a formal request to attend an event.
- **reception:** the party or celebration after the wedding ceremony.
- **ceremony:** the formal event where the marriage takes place.
- **etiquette:** the rules of polite behavior in a society.
- **guests:** the people invited to the event.

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: review_modals -->

*(Note: In this lesson, the focus shifts towards Listening, Writing, and applying modals contextually).*

**Applying Modals for Etiquette**
When talking about etiquette and customs, we often use modals of intention and request.
- *I will send the invitations next week.* (Intention)
- *Could you please RSVP by Friday?* (Request)

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Match the words to their definitions:
   a. groom
   b. reception
   c. etiquette
   d. bride
   
   1. The rules of polite behavior.
   2. The party after the wedding.
   3. The woman getting married.
   4. The man getting married.

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Listening Skill - Listen for main ideas:** Have students listen to a conversation about wedding etiquette. Ask them to identify the main cultural rules being discussed before listening for specific details.
**Writing:** Have students write a short paragraph about wedding customs in their own country. Encourage them to use vocabulary from the lesson and practice sequencing words.
**Language and Life:** Discuss Global Competence. Understanding and appreciating social customs is key to intercultural communication. Have students compare a local social event with one from a different culture.

### Answer Key
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: answer_key -->

**Practice (Wedding Customs):**
a - 4
b - 2
c - 1
d - 3

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

Wedding etiquette differs drastically around the world. In some cultures, wearing white as a guest is a major faux pas, while in others, specific colors represent mourning or bad luck. Exploring these customs helps build global competence and cultural empathy.
