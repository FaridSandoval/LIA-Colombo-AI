# Unit 12 — I'm a Member

## Lesson 1 — Different Groups

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Types of Community & Networking**
Read the definitions to learn words related to professional and social groups:
- **network:** to meet people who might be useful to know, especially in your job (verb).
- **belong to:** to be a member of a group or organization (verb).
- **achieve:** to succeed in finishing something or reaching an aim, especially after a lot of work (verb).
- **aim:** a result that your plans or actions are intended to achieve (noun).
- **chat:** to talk with someone in a friendly and informal way (verb).
- **share:** to tell someone else about your thoughts, feelings, or ideas (verb).
- **community of practice:** a group of people who share a concern or a passion for something they do and learn how to do it better as they interact regularly (noun).

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: reflexive_pronouns -->

**Reflexive Pronouns**
We use reflexive pronouns when the subject and the object of the verb are the same person or thing.
- **Singular:** *myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself*.
- **Plural:** *ourselves, yourselves, themselves*.

*Examples:*
- I taught **myself** how to code.
- They are enjoying **themselves** at the conference.
- She introduced **herself** to the manager.

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Choose the correct vocabulary word to complete the sentences:
   a. The best thing about going to conferences is the chance to (network / share). I love meeting new people.
   b. It's important to (belong to / network) a group of people who have the same interests as you.
   c. You need to really work hard to (achieve / belong to) your goal of becoming the managing director.
   d. The (aim / chat) of this meeting is to decide how we can save money for the company.
   e. I have a few ideas that I'd like to (share / chat) with you, if that's OK.

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Reading Skill - Understand the main ideas:** Have students read the article "Different Groups". Remind them that when we read for the main ideas, we read to understand the key points the writer is making, and we skip over small details or words we don't understand.
**Vocabulary:** Ask students what communities of practice or social groups they belong to (e.g., sports clubs, online gaming forums, study groups).
**Grammar Activity:** Have students write three things they can do by themselves without any help using reflexive pronouns (e.g., *I can cook a meal for myself*).

### Answer Key
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: answer_key -->

**Practice (Networking Vocabulary):**
a. network
b. belong to
c. achieve
d. aim
e. share

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

The concept of "networking" is highly emphasized in professional cultures, especially in the US and the UK. It is often said that "it's not what you know, but who you know." Teaching students how to network and introduce themselves confidently is a key professional skill.

## Lesson 2 — Expanding Your Network

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Social Groups**
Words and phrases to describe different types of organizations and social interactions:
- **social group:** two or more people who interact with one another and share similar characteristics.
- **subculture:** a cultural group within a larger culture, often having beliefs or interests at variance with those of the larger culture.
- **youth tribe:** a youth subculture with distinct styles, behaviors, and interests.
- **interact:** to communicate with or react to each other.
- **expand:** to increase in size, number, or importance.

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: review_of_tenses -->

**Review of Tenses**
Throughout this course, you have learned several tenses to describe the past, present, and future. 
- **Simple Present:** For habits and facts (*I belong to a club*).
- **Simple Past:** For finished actions in the past (*I joined the club last year*).
- **Past Progressive:** For actions in progress in the past (*I was chatting with my friend when the meeting started*).
- **Present Perfect:** For life experiences and recent events (*I have just achieved my goal*).
- **Future Forms:** For predictions and plans (*I am going to expand my network next year*).

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Identify the tense of each sentence:
   a. She has already met the new boss. ____________________
   b. We usually share our ideas at the weekly meeting. ____________________
   c. They were talking about the project when the client arrived. ____________________
   d. I will probably join the community of practice soon. ____________________
   e. He started his own business in 2023. ____________________

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Listening Skill - Listen for the main ideas:** Have students listen to a podcast about networking. Ask them to identify the speakers' main advice on how to expand a professional network.
**Grammar Review:** Use this lesson to review the most challenging tenses for your students. Create a timeline on the board and place sentences on it to visually remind students of the differences between the Simple Past and the Present Perfect.
**Follow A Pro:** If using the video/reading, explore the career of an "Entrepreneur". Discuss how networking is a crucial skill for entrepreneurs to find investors and clients.

### Answer Key
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: answer_key -->

**Practice (Review of Tenses):**
a. Present Perfect
b. Simple Present
c. Past Progressive (and Simple Past)
d. Future (Prediction / Possibility)
e. Simple Past

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

Entrepreneurs and start-up cultures are celebrated globally. Events like "mixers," "hackathons," and "pitch nights" are modern social groups where the primary goal is rapid, effective networking and sharing innovative ideas.

## Lesson 3 — Youth Tribes and Subcultures

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Appearance and Subcultures**
Words used to describe the physical appearance and style of different groups:
- **costume:** a set of clothes in a style typical of a particular country or historical period, or suitable for a particular activity.
- **make-up:** cosmetics applied to the face to enhance or alter appearance.
- **bright colors:** strong, easily noticed colors.
- **character:** a person represented in a movie, play, or story.
- **cosplay:** the practice of dressing up as a character from a movie, book, or video game.

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: writing_a_description -->

*(Note: In this lesson, the focus shifts towards Writing and Speaking using descriptive language).*

**Writing a Description of a Subculture**
When writing a description, it is important to organize your ideas logically:
- **First:** Give background information and a little history about the group.
- **Second:** Describe what the people look like and what they do (Appearance and habits).
- **Third:** Give your personal opinion about the subculture.

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Match the paragraph function to its content based on the writing rules above:
   a. "In my opinion, cosplaying is a great way to meet people with the same interests." _____
   b. "A Japanese reporter invented the term cosplay using the words costume and play in 1984." _____
   c. "Cosplayers wear bright colors and often wear make-up to make them look like their favorite characters." _____

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Writing:** Have students choose a youth subculture (e.g., skaters, gamers, goths, cosplayers, K-pop fans) and write a short description in three paragraphs following the structure taught in the lesson.
**Speaking - Confident Communicator:** Teach students how to further a point in a discussion. To add a point, say: *Also... / In addition to that... / Not only that, but... also...* (e.g., *You can express your personality in a subculture. Not only that, but you can also meet people.*).
**Language and Life - Media Literacy:** Set up a collaborative task where students design an ad campaign to reach a specific target group or subculture. They need to decide what images, language, and platforms will appeal to that specific community.

### Answer Key
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: answer_key -->

**Practice (Writing a Description):**
a. Third (Personal opinion)
b. First (Background information)
c. Second (Physical description)

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

Subcultures like "Cosplay" originated largely from Japanese pop culture and sci-fi/comic conventions in the US, but have become a massive global phenomenon. These tribes transcend geographical borders, connecting people globally through shared aesthetics and passions.
