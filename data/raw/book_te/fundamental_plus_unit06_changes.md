# Unit 6 — Changes

## Lesson 1 — Status Quo

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Status Quo**
Read the definitions to learn words related to risk, change, and keeping things the same:
- **status quo:** the present situation or the way things usually are (noun).
- **change:** to make or become different (verb).
- **risk:** a possibility of something bad happening (noun).
- **routine:** a regular way of doing things in a particular order (noun).
- **comfortable:** feeling relaxed and not worried (adjective).
- **avoid:** to stay away from someone or something (verb).

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: present_perfect_for_since -->

**Present Perfect with *for* and *since***
We use the present perfect to talk about actions or situations that started in the past and continue to the present.
- Use **for** + a period of time (e.g., *for two years, for three months, for a long time*).
- Use **since** + a specific point in time in the past (e.g., *since 2018, since last Tuesday, since I was a child*).

*Examples:*
- I have lived in this city **for** five years.
- She has worked at the company **since** January.

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Complete the sentences using *for* or *since*:
   a. We have been friends _____ we were in elementary school.
   b. He hasn't eaten any junk food _____ three weeks.
   c. They have known about the new project _____ yesterday.
   d. I have had this smartphone _____ a couple of years.
   e. The status quo hasn't changed _____ the 1990s.

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Reading (Optional):** Explain the concept of "status quo bias" (the preference to keep things the way they are rather than taking a risk to change them). Have students discuss changes in their lives over the last year.
**Grammar Activity:** Draw a timeline on the board. Mark a specific start date (e.g., 2020) and a duration (e.g., 6 years). Visually show how *since* points to the start date and *for* measures the distance from the start to now.
**Speaking:** Have students debate changes in their town. Do they view change as a positive or negative thing in their life?

### Answer Key
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: answer_key -->

**Practice (Present Perfect with for/since):**
a. since
b. for
c. since
d. for
e. since

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

The phrase "status quo" comes from Latin. In many professional and academic environments, challenging the status quo is seen as a sign of leadership and innovation, though it naturally involves taking risks.

## Lesson 2 — Bucket List

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Life Experiences**
Learn the past participles of these irregular verbs to talk about life experiences:
- **be** -> been
- **do** -> done
- **eat** -> eaten
- **go** -> gone
- **hear** -> heard
- **meet** -> met
- **ride** -> ridden
- **run** -> run
- **sing** -> sung
- **visit** -> visited

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: present_perfect_ever_never -->

**Present Perfect with *ever* and *never***
We use the present perfect with *ever* and *never* to talk about life experiences (actions that happened at an unspecified time before now).
- Use **ever** in questions to ask if someone has done something in their life.
- Use **never** in affirmative sentences to give a negative meaning (meaning "not at any time in my life").

*Examples:*
- Have you **ever** flown in a helicopter?
- I have **never** driven a sports car.

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Complete the email with the past participles of verbs from the vocabulary box (*be, do, eat, go, hear, ride, run, sing, visit*):
   "Hey John, I like your idea of making a bucket list. There are a lot of things I want to do. I've never 1 _____ to Africa. I've 2 _____ my cousins in Spain, and I've 3 _____ to the capital of my country. I've never 4 _____ bungee jumping or 5 _____ a horse. I am sure I've never 6 _____ Korean food, and I've 7 _____ that it's really good! I like to sing, but I've never 8 _____ in public. I've 9 _____ a few 5K races, but I've never 10 _____ a marathon."

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Vocabulary Activity:** Write the base forms of the verbs on the board and have students race to write the simple past and past participle forms.
**Listening Skill - Listen for main ideas:** Tell students to listen to the podcast about bucket lists and number the tips in the correct order. Remind them to identify key words.
**Confident Communicator:** Teach phrases to react to information (e.g., *Really? Wow! I've never done that!*).
**Thinking Skill - Create:** Write two big things and two smaller things for your personal bucket list. Share ideas with a partner and give reasons for the choices.

<!-- topic: answer_key -->

2. visited
3. been (or gone)
4. done
5. ridden

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

A "bucket list" is a list of things a person hopes to do or experience during their lifetime. The term was popularized by the 2007 American comedy-drama film *The Bucket List*. Discussing bucket lists is a great way to talk about future goals and life experiences.

## Lesson 3 — Changing Cities

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Changing Cities**
Words to describe changes and development in urban areas:
- **construct:** to build something, such as a building or bridge.
- **renovate:** to repair and improve a building so it looks new.
- **tear down:** to destroy a building completely.
- **expand:** to make something larger.
- **neighborhood:** an area of a town or city where people live.
- **development:** the process of growing or changing.

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: present_perfect_review -->

*(Note: In this lesson the focus shifts towards Listening, Speaking, and recycling the Present Perfect in context).*

**Talking About Life Experiences and Changes**
Use the present perfect to describe changes that have occurred over time in a place you know well.
- *They have constructed a new mall.*
- *The neighborhood has expanded since 2015.*
- *Have you ever visited the new historic center?*

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Choose the correct word to complete each sentence:
   a. The city plans to (tear down / construct) the old, unsafe factory.
   b. They are going to (expand / renovate) the historic museum to make it look new again.
   c. The new housing (neighborhood / development) has brought many new families to the area.
   d. My (status quo / neighborhood) has changed a lot since I was a child.

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Listening:** Have students listen to a radio show about changing cities. Ask them to listen for specific information about what has been built or torn down.
**Language and Life - Collaboration:** Set up a collaborative task where students list recommendations for visitors discovering their town. They should discuss what has changed and what is worth seeing.
**Follow A Pro (Architecture):** If using the video, have students watch an urban planner or ecological architect to learn about the job. Discuss: *How can architects help make buildings more ecological? Have you been in any buildings that are "green"?*

### Answer Key
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: answer_key -->

**Practice (Changing Cities):**
a. tear down
b. renovate
c. development
d. neighborhood

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

Urban development is a major topic worldwide. In many historical cities, there is an ongoing debate between tearing down old buildings to modernize versus renovating them to preserve cultural heritage. Comparing how different countries handle historic architecture provides excellent discussion material.
