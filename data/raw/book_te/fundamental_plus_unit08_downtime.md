# Unit 8 — Downtime

## Lesson 1 — Kidult Activities

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Leisure and "Kidult" Activities**
Read the definitions and phrases to learn vocabulary related to hobbies and free time:
- **building airplanes:** making model aircraft for fun.
- **flying kites:** an outdoor activity usually done on a windy day.
- **playing and watching sports:** participating in or viewing athletic games.
- **taking pictures:** doing photography.
- **using colored pencils:** drawing or coloring in books (a popular "kidult" activity).
- **bringing a dish:** preparing and taking food to a shared meal or party.

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: gerund_phrases -->

**Gerund Phrases as Subject and Object**
A gerund is the *-ing* form of a verb acting as a noun. A gerund phrase includes the gerund and its modifiers or objects.
- **As a Subject:** The gerund phrase acts as the main noun doing the action in the sentence.
  *Example:* **Building model airplanes** is my dad's favorite hobby.
- **As an Object:** The gerund phrase receives the action of the main verb.
  *Example:* I really enjoy **taking pictures** at the park.

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Complete the sentences using the vocabulary words in gerund form:
   a. __________ (play) sports is a great way to stay healthy.
   b. Everyone is going to __________ (bring) a dish to the big meal.
   c. It's windy today, which means it's a great day for __________ (fly) kites.
   d. She loves __________ (use) colored pencils to relax after work.

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Vocabulary:** Introduce the concept of "kidult" activities—activities traditionally associated with children that adults enjoy doing to relieve stress (e.g., adult coloring books, building models, flying kites). 
**Grammar Activity:** Have students write two sentences about their own hobbies, one using a gerund phrase as the subject and one using it as the object.
**Speaking:** Have students discuss what "kidult" activities are popular in their country. Do they think it's acceptable for adults to do these activities?

### Answer Key
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: answer_key -->

**Practice (Gerund Phrases):**
a. Playing
b. bring
c. flying
d. using

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

The "kidult" trend has grown significantly worldwide. Adult coloring books, collecting action figures, or building complex LEGO sets are no longer seen as just for kids, but as legitimate ways for adults to practice mindfulness and disconnect from daily stress.

## Lesson 2 — How Are You Feeling?

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Adjectives of Feeling**
Words to describe how you feel during your downtime or at work:
- **stressed:** worried because you have too much to do.
- **bored:** feeling unhappy because you are not doing anything and have nothing to do.
- **rested:** feeling healthy and full of energy because you slept a lot or relaxed.
- **guilty:** feeling bad because you did something wrong or because you are resting when you think you should be working.

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: verb_gerund_infinitive -->

**Verb + Gerund or Infinitive**
Some verbs are followed by an infinitive (to + verb), some by a gerund (verb + -ing), and some can take either.
- **Verbs followed by Infinitives:** *decide, hope, plan, learn, would like*.
  *Example:* We're **learning to create** art.
- **Verbs followed by Gerunds:** *enjoy, finish, mind*.
  *Example:* I **enjoy playing** sports.
- **Verbs followed by either (with no change in meaning):** *like, love, prefer*.
  *Example:* I **love to sleep** / I **love sleeping**.
- **Would rather:** Followed by the base form of the verb (without "to").
  *Example:* I **'d rather go** on Wednesday.

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Choose the correct adjective of feeling to complete the sentences:
   a. I'm worried about work because I have too much to do. I'm really (bored / guilty / stressed).
   b. I'm not doing anything, and my friends are all busy. I'm (bored / stressed / rested).
   c. I had a great weekend. I saw friends and slept a lot. I feel very (guilty / bored / rested).
   d. My brother always leaves the party first to go study. He feels (stressed / guilty / rested) if he doesn't do his homework.

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Vocabulary:** Ask students to give examples of situations that make them feel stressed, bored, rested, or guilty. 
**Grammar:** Create a three-column chart on the board (Infinitive, Gerund, Both). Hand out flashcards with verbs and have students place them in the correct column. Remind them that *would rather* is a special case that takes the base form.
**Confident Communicator:** Teach phrases to agree with a proposal (e.g., *That sounds like a great idea. I'd love to do that.*).

### Answer Key
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: answer_key -->

**Practice (Adjectives of feeling):**
a. stressed
b. bored
c. rested
d. guilty

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

In many fast-paced cultures, taking downtime can actually make people feel "guilty" because society highly values constant productivity. This is why "mindfulness" and scheduled leisure activities have become so necessary to combat burnout.

## Lesson 3 — The Psychology of Doing Nothing

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Relaxation and Mindfulness**
Terms used when talking about mental health and downtime:
- **mindfulness:** the practice of being aware of your body, mind, and feelings in the present moment.
- **downtime:** a period of time when you relax and do not do much.
- **burnout:** extreme tiredness usually caused by working too much.
- **unplug:** to stop using electronic devices to relax.

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: grammar_review -->

*(Note: In this lesson, the focus shifts towards Listening, Speaking, and applying Gerunds and Infinitives contextually).*

**Expressing Opinions and Making Plans**
When discussing plans or opinions about downtime, combine gerunds, infinitives, and *would rather*.
- *I prefer **doing** nothing on Sundays.*
- *We plan **to go** to the park tomorrow.*
- *I would rather **unplug** and read a book than watch TV.*

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Complete the email with the correct form of the verbs in parentheses (infinitive, gerund, or base form):
   "Hi Susie,
   You know I love 1 __________ (make) models. Would you like 2 __________ (go) to a class with me? We're learning 3 __________ (create) art with different materials. I 4 __________ (rather / go) on Wednesday if that's OK. Let me know!"

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Listening:** Have students listen to a class discussion about the psychology of doing nothing. Ask them to listen for the reasons why doing nothing is actually good for the brain.
**Speaking:** Set up a group discussion. Have students explain a concept from the listening or reading. Encourage them to explain why enjoyable kids' activities (kidult activities) reduce burnout.
**Language and Life - Mindfulness:** Have students select a series of calming activities and create a "Mindfulness Plan" for their weekends to relax their minds.

### Answer Key
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: answer_key -->

**Practice (Making Plans):**
1. making (or to make)
2. to go
3. to create
4. 'd rather go (would rather go)

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

"Doing nothing" (sometimes referred to in Italian as *il dolce far niente*, the sweetness of doing nothing) is viewed very differently across cultures. Learning to appreciate quiet moments without electronic distractions is a growing psychological movement in the modern digital age.
