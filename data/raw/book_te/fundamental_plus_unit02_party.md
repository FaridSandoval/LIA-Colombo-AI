# Unit 2 — Party!

## Lesson 1 — Days of the Year

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Holidays**
Learn these words related to days of the year and celebrations:
- **official:** agreed upon or arranged by people in power (adjective)
- **calendar:** a chart with days of the year showing seasonal information (noun)
- **get the day off:** when you don't have to go to work or school (phrase)
- **celebrate:** do something to recognize an important or special event (verb)
- **boss:** a person who is in charge at work (noun)
- **arrange:** make plans for an event (verb)
- **receive:** get something from someone (verb)
- **colleague:** a person you work with (noun)

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: quantifiers -->

**Quantifiers**
We use quantifiers to talk about amounts. 
- Use **a little**, **too much**, and **a lot of** / **some** / **any** with *uncountable nouns* (e.g., time, advice).
- Use **a few**, **too many**, and **a lot of** / **some** / **any** with *countable nouns* (e.g., dates, selfies).

*Examples:*
- There's **too much** time spent taking selfies.
- I learned there are **a lot of** days to celebrate.
- There are **a few** websites that list all these celebrations.

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Complete the recipe text with the correct quantifiers:
   *Pasteles de hoja* are a popular dish in Puerto Rico. They are usually made around Christmas and families often spend 1 (a little / a few) _____ days preparing them. It's common to make 2 (a lot of / a few) _____ pasteles at the same time (usually between 50 and 200!). Fill the dough with 3 (some / a few) _____ meat. Don't add 4 (too many / too much) _____ meat because you have to add the chickpeas, raisins, and olives, too. People usually freeze them or take 5 (a little / some) _____ boxes of pasteles as a gift. They are delicious, but very big, so you may not want to eat 6 (too much / too many) _____!

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Vocabulary:** Have students broaden their knowledge of the words by encouraging them to look up synonyms in an online or monolingual dictionary. 
**Grammar:** Remind students that *some* and *any* can be used when the exact number or amount is not known. They can be used with both countable and uncountable nouns. Explain that countable nouns are known as unit nouns and can be plural, while uncountable nouns are mass nouns and have to be divided up or measured.
**Thinking Skill - Analyze:** Have students work in groups to research national holidays in different countries. Ask them to discuss inferences that can be made about a country based on the holidays that are observed.

### Answer Key
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: answer_key -->

**Practice (Quantifiers):**
1. a few
2. a lot of
3. some
4. too much
5. some
6. too many

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

People around the world celebrate both official and unofficial holidays. For example, Japan celebrates Health and Sports Day on October 8. There are also quirky, unofficial days celebrated globally, like World Sleep Day, World Yoga Day, and Make Music Day. Discussing these can be a fun way to explore different cultures' values.

## Lesson 2 — The Business of Holidays

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Money and Shopping**
Phrases to talk about how we use money:
- **make money:** get money by working or selling something.
- **spend money on:** buy things (e.g., trips abroad, gifts).
- **lend money:** give someone money because they don't have enough.
- **waste money on:** buy things you don't need.
- **overspend:** spend more money than you have.
- **borrow money:** ask someone to give you money because you don't have enough.

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: enough_not_enough -->

**Enough / Not enough**
- **Enough** shows that the amount is the right amount.
- **Not enough** shows that the amount is not the right amount.
- Both can be used with *countable* and *uncountable* nouns.

*Examples:*
- I have **enough** decorations. (Countable)
- They don't have **enough** money. (Uncountable)

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Write sentences using *enough* or *not enough* based on the prompts:
   a. I / have enough / money / buy / this hat. (You only have $5 but the hat is $25)
      ______________________________________________________
   b. There / be enough / eggs / make / the cake. (You need 4 eggs but only have 2)
      ______________________________________________________
   c. She / have enough / food / on her plate. (Her plate is completely full)
      ______________________________________________________
   d. He / have enough / casual / shoes. (He has 10 pairs of shoes)
      ______________________________________________________

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Vocabulary:** Have students practice the money phrases by categorizing them into two columns: Money coming in (*make money, borrow money*) and Money going out (*spend money on, lend money, waste money on, overspend*). 
**Make it Real:** Teach students informal synonyms for money in English, such as: *cash, bucks, loot, bread, dough*.
**Grammar Additional Activity:** Play "Why Don't You...?" Tell students to ask each other hypothetical questions starting with "Why don't you..." and answer with *enough* or *not enough*. (e.g., "Why don't you drive a Ferrari?" -> "I don't have enough money.")

### Answer Key
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: answer_key -->

**Practice (Enough / Not enough):**
a. I don't have enough money to buy this hat.
b. There aren't enough eggs to make the cake.
c. She has enough food on her plate.
d. He has enough casual shoes.

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

The way people spend money on holidays can vary. In many cultures, celebrations like Thanksgiving or Christmas involve significant spending on food, gifts, and decorations. Discussing alternatives to overspending (like making homemade gifts or baking cookies) is a great way to talk about financial responsibility and cultural expectations.

## Lesson 3 — Celebration Food and Drink

### Vocabulary
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: vocabulary -->

**Food and Drink Packaging**
Words to describe how food and drinks are packaged:
- a **bag** of (oranges / green onions)
- a **bottle** of (water / soy sauce)
- a **box** of (chocolates / mushrooms)
- a **can** of (nuts / water chestnuts)
- a **carton** of (juice / eggs)
- a **jar** of (olives / sweet chili sauce)
- a **jug** of (apple cider)
- a **package** of (cookies / dumpling wrappers)

### Grammar
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: grammar -->
<!-- grammar_focus: linking_words -->

**Linking Words for Writing**
When writing about a celebration, use linking words to connect your ideas:
- **but:** introduces a contrasting idea.
- **and:** adds extra information.
- **because:** gives a reason.
- **so:** gives a result.
- **or:** gives an alternative.

### Practice
<!-- audience: student -->
<!-- topic: practice -->

1. Choose the correct packaging word for each item:
   a. Can you buy a (carton / jar) of juice at the store?
   b. I need a (bag / can) of tomatoes for the soup.
   c. She received a beautiful (box / jug) of chocolates for her birthday.
   d. We drank a whole (package / bottle) of water after the run.
   e. He opened a (jar / bag) of olives for the party.

### Teacher Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: pedagogy -->

**Vocabulary Extra Practice:** For more practice naming containers, have students name other things that are bought in bags, boxes, cartons, jugs, bottles, cans, jars, and packages (e.g., bag of apples, box of cereal, carton of milk, jug of water, can of tuna).
**Pronunciation:** Focus on the difference between the /tʃ/ sound (chop, cheap) and the /ʃ/ sound (shop, sheep). Tell students to pay particular attention to the position of the tongue and the flow of air.
**Writing/Speaking:** Have students think about a celebration food they like. Ask them to list the ingredients using the container vocabulary (e.g., "To make pavlova, you need a carton of eggs, a carton of cream...").

### Cultural Notes
<!-- audience: teacher_only -->
<!-- topic: cultural_context -->

The text mentions the *Grand Kadooment Day* at the end of the *Crop Over* festival in Barbados, a celebration to mark the end of the sugar cane season. Using this as a model helps students see how specific foods (like fried fish and snow-cones) are tied to local heritage and history.
